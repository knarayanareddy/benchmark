02

Doorway — the UNICRI product, specified

One-line definition

A free, self-serve install that turns on the detection tools a small platform would never find, puts a gate in front of the search rather than a banner beside it, changes shape every time it fires, and hands the person to the right help service in the right language — without the platform, or anyone else, learning anything about who they are.

Why this shape and not another

Three constraints from the evidence force it, and it is worth being able to recite them because all three will be challenged in Q&A.

  • Detection is free and solved; intervention is neither. Seven free tools exist to find an illegal image (Cloudflare CSAM Scanning, Project Arachnid Shield, PhotoDNA, IWF Image Intercept, Tech Coalition Pathways, Google CSAI Match, NCMEC reporting) and not one interruptable tool exists to intercept the person. Bundling detection is not padding — it is the reason a moderator installs anything at all.
  • Choice architecture beats copy by two orders of magnitude. Ahmia 12.44% versus Pornhub 0.0175% is a 700-fold spread across deployments with comparable messages. The variable that moves is how much else is on the screen.
  • Repetition destroys the effect, and nobody has built against it. reThink's referral rate declined across its 18-month deployment; the dual-process model puts the per-warning optimum near 18 days and predicts that variety helps only where habituation is variant-specific. No deployed intervention rotates.

The three parts

The Door

client · 14 KB · no framework
What the person sees
Behaviour
Renders in one of five formats, chosen automatically by platform type and exposure count — never configured by hand. Gate (full interstitial, results withheld until dismissed), Blur (results visible but unreadable until acknowledged), Card (inline above results), Chat (three-turn conversation, button-driven, escalating to text), Quiet (a single line, used only for fifth-plus exposures of the least severe trigger class).
Accessibility
ARIA dialog with focus trap, ESC dismissal, reduced-motion support, full keyboard path, 4.5:1 contrast, and a screen-reader announcement that does not read the word "police" aloud unexpectedly.
Content
Message variants supplied by the routes bundle. Four-message structure from Project Intercept, adopted verbatim: it is a crime · it harms a child · there are consequences for you · there is a way out. Dutch and English, with the AI-CSAM block (§08 finding 4) included as a sixth variant nobody else ships.
What it never does
Never reads the query text — the platform tells it that a trigger fired, never what was typed. Never sets a cookie. Never calls a third party. Never records a keystroke, a dwell time, or a scroll position.

The Shift

scheduler · deterministic · zero state transmitted
Why the fifth time looks different from the first
What it does
Picks which variant and which format to show, using a counter held in the browser's own localStorage and never sent anywhere. On the first exposure it gates. On the third it changes message family. On the fifth it changes form — gate to chat — rather than repeating a sentence more loudly.
The model behind it
Watters, Scanlan & Jain (2026): sensitisation accumulates over ~50 days while attention depletes ~30% per exposure and recovers over ~10 days, producing an interior per-warning optimum around 18 days. The Shift enforces a minimum re-warning interval and rotates within that envelope.
Why it is the differentiator
It is the only part of any submission that answers a question the brief itself asks — "how do you avoid habituation when someone sees it for the fifth time?" — with a model, a parameter and a demonstrable mechanic rather than an adjective.
Honest limit
τ*≈18 days is a calibration from a mathematical model, not a validated scheduling rule. The product should ship the parameter as configurable and say exactly that on the settings screen. Honesty here is worth more than confidence.

The Routes

data · versioned · signed · updatable without a redeploy
The part that actually helps anyone
What it is
A JSON bundle keyed by language, then country — not country alone. Each entry carries: service name, channel (self-help / chat / phone), hours, languages actually spoken, anonymity terms, and a one-sentence statement of what the service does and does not report.
Why language-first
Because a Dutch forum has English-speaking users, and telling an English speaker to call a Dutch helpline is worse than telling them nothing. Dutch UI language routes to Offlimits preventielijn; anything else routes to ReDirection (six languages) and then Stop It Now UK.
What it fixes
Seven in ten people who want help cannot find an anonymous route to it (Insoll 2024, n=4,493), and 73.9% of those who looked could not get help (Nurmi 2024, n=176,683 domains). The bundle is a maintained, honest version of the thing they were failing to find.
The capacity rule
Every route carries a capacity flag. Self-help is the default. Human channels appear only where the exposure pattern or the user's own selection indicates higher need. A module that sends everyone to a phone line would break the phone line.

What it is not

Tempting but wrongWhy
A chatbot that counselsreThink's own evaluation found its chatbot handled free text badly — 132 of ~1,650 conversations were exportable, and 80% of users used buttons. A hackathon chatbot will be worse than reThink's, and therapy-adjacent copy is a liability. Buttons, then a human.
A classifier that spots abusersThat is profiling, it is what the "no private data" requirement forbids, and at hackathon accuracy it will flag survivors, journalists and parents.
A reporting-tool competitorOfflimits is a Trusted Flagger and NCMEC reporting is free. Nothing to add.
A dashboard-first productThe dashboard is what makes it adoptable, not what makes it work. If the demo spends more than 40 seconds on charts, the pitch is upside down.
An age-verification layerPolitically live in the UK and the EU, and technically a different product with a different threat model. Mention it as adjacent, don't build it.

04

No private data — and still measurable

This is the hardest requirement you've set, and it is the one worth getting exactly right, because the field is full of people who say "privacy-preserving" and then ship a session ID.

The contradiction, stated properly

To prove a warning changed behaviour you must observe a person before and after. To observe a person you must know which person. To know which person destroys the anonymity that makes them click at all — 27.2% of CSAM users say they are afraid to seek help, and anonymity is the thing they cite. This is not a resource problem. It is a logical bind, and every design that ignores it is lying somewhere.

There are exactly four ways out, and only one of them is good:

Escape routeWorks?Why
Track individuals, promise not to lookNoThe data exists, so it can be subpoenaed, breached, or joined. A promise is not a control.
Track nothing, claim nothingWeakHonest but unfundable. The platform cannot justify keeping it installed and UNICRI cannot show its own funders anything.
Differential privacy on individual eventsPartialCorrect but over-engineered at these volumes, and it still requires a per-user key to add noise to a person's own stream.
Aggregate only, with the unit of inference moved from the person to the platformYesAll the causal power, none of the identification. This is the design below.

The design: the browser remembers, the network never sees

Everything that requires knowing this person happens inside their own browser and never leaves it. Everything that leaves is a count that thousands of people share.

What stays on the device

// localStorage — never transmitted, never readable by the network
{
  "n": 5,                                  // this browser has seen 5 triggers
  "seen": ["harm-02", "legal-01", "ctrl-03"],   // which variants, for rotation
  "last": 1762432000                       // timestamp of the last one, for the interval rule
}

This is what makes the Shift work. It is also what makes the habituation curve measurable — and it never crosses a network boundary. Any user can read it, delete it, or disable it.

What leaves the device

POST /v1/c                     // 8 fields. No session. No IP. No UA. No referrer. No query.
{
  "i": "inst_7f3a2c",          // install id — one per PLATFORM, not per person
  "v": "harm-02",              // which message variant was shown
  "f": "gate",                 // which format was shown
  "t": "search",               // trigger class only ('search'|'chat'|'nav'|'upload')
  "o": "clicked_help",         // one of five outcomes
  "r": "nl-offlimits",         // which route was offered
  "e": "5+",                   // exposure BUCKET, never the exact count
  "b": 14                      // hour of day, 0–23 — no date, no minute
}
The deliberate departure from the organisers' own contract

The starter kit's PLUGIN_CONTRACT.md specifies a sessionScopedId — a random identifier, different every session, never persisted. That is far better than a user ID, and it is still the wrong choice, for a reason that is easy to state and hard to answer: a random identifier stops being anonymous the moment it appears twice. Two events sharing an ID inside one session link those two events. Three sessions sharing an install id link the sessions. The kit's own privacy note says the ID is never persisted — but the aggregation layer necessarily holds it for the duration of a session, which is exactly the window in which linkage happens.

Doorway removes it entirely. The exposure bucket e carries the only longitudinal information the product actually needs — how many times this browser has seen the module — and it is coarse enough to be shared by millions of people. We can build the habituation curve without ever being able to draw a line between two events. That sentence is worth a minute of the pitch on its own.

How the platform measures impact anyway

Four tiers, ascending in causal strength. The platform chooses how far up the ladder to go, and the first three require nothing from anyone.

Tier 1 — Presence

Automatic · zero data
"Is it on, and is it firing?"
Measures
Install id, version, last heartbeat, total trigger count, format mix, outcome mix. All aggregate, all non-identifying.
What it answers
Did anybody install this and leave it on? Is it reaching the moments it was built for, or is the trigger misconfigured and firing never?
Why it matters
The most common failure of every safety tool ever shipped to small platforms is silent non-operation. A heartbeat catches it in a day.

Tier 2 — Reach and routing

Automatic · aggregate only
"Who is it getting to, and where are they sent?"
Measures
Triggers per thousand searches. Click-through by variant × format × trigger × exposure bucket. Route mix.
What it answers
Is the format change doing what the model says? Does the fifth exposure still convert, or has it become wallpaper? Which route gets chosen when a real person is offered three?
The headline number
Click-through rate with an honest denominator — the thing reThink published as 490-in-2.8-million and Intercept as 700K-in-70-million. Doorway's dashboard shows it the same way, unprompted, including the exposure-bucket breakdown the others never published.

Tier 3 — Arrival

Requires one cooperation · still no personal data
"Did anyone actually get help?"
How
The route URL carries a source tag that the receiving service counts in the ordinary course of its work — offlimits.nl/?src=doorway. Offlimits already counts where its contacts come from. They report back a single number: "N contacts arrived via Doorway this month."
What it answers
The only outcome that matters. A click is not help. 68 conversations from 2.8 million warnings is the number that reframes the entire field, and it is only visible at the service end.
What it costs
One line in a monthly email from a partner organisation. No data about any individual crosses in either direction.

Tier 4 — Causal inference

Opt-in study mode · the only real answer
"Does any of this work?" — the question the systematic review says nobody has answered
The design
Stepped-wedge cluster randomisation at the platform level. Twenty platforms agree to install; ten go live in week 1, ten in week 9. Compare trigger-level and service-level outcomes across the transition, within each platform and between cohorts.
Why it works without individual data
The unit of randomisation is the platform, not the person. Nobody is randomised, followed, or observed. The comparison is between a forum that has the module and a forum that does not yet — which is a difference between two websites, not between two people.
What it can and cannot say
It can say whether installing the module changes the rate at which people reach help services. It cannot say what any individual did. That is the correct trade, and it is a stronger design than anything in the published literature except Ahmia's, which randomised messages within one platform rather than platforms.
Why most platforms won't do it
Because a study is a commitment and the whole reason the install is easy is that it isn't one. So don't force it. Ship the module free to everyone and the study as a separate opt-in — and say plainly in the pitch that the study is the part nobody has run yet.

What this design can and cannot prove

QuestionAnswerable?How
How many people saw it, and what did they do?YesCounts by variant, format, trigger and exposure bucket.
Does effectiveness decay with repetition?YesThe exposure bucket gives a population-level habituation curve — the first one anyone would have published.
Which message and format works better?YesVariant assignment is deterministic per install, so platforms differ in exposure mix; with enough installs this is a natural experiment, and study mode makes it a real one.
Did this person's behaviour change?NoBy design. This is a feature, not a limitation — following people up would destroy the product.
Did anyone get help?PartlyService-side counts, with self-report attribution. Honest, imperfect, and the same method reThink used.
Was the same person counted twice?NoCannot be determined — and at these base rates individual-level rates would be statistically meaningless anyway. Say so before a juror says it.
The line to use in the pitch

"Every other deployment in this field measures clicks and hopes they mean help. We measure the doors and the arrivals, we cannot identify a single person who walked through, and that is why they walk through."

05

Doorway: the information flow, end to end

Every step below says who holds what. The point of the diagram is the middle column: what crosses which boundary.

Who holds what, at rest

The person's browser
  • The query they typed — already there
  • The local counter and variant list
  • The rendered message
  • Nothing else
The platform
  • Its own search logs — already its data
  • That a trigger fired, never why
  • Its own counters, in its own database
  • Its install id
Doorway infrastructure
  • Aggregate counts by variant / format / bucket
  • Install ids and heartbeats
  • No IPs retained (edge does not log)
  • Nothing joinable to a person

The eleven steps

Person · browser

1 · A search is entered

The person types into the platform's own search box. The string exists in the browser and, after submission, in the platform's log. Doorway is not yet involved.

Platform · server or edge

2 · The platform decides a trigger fired

Two modes. Edge mode: the Cloudflare Worker matches the query against its own blocklist — the protected list never leaves Cloudflare, and Doorway is not in this step at all. App mode: the platform's own search handler calls doorway.trigger({type:'search', route:'blocked'}). Either way the module receives a boolean, never a string.

This is the load-bearing privacy decision. Most designs would pass the query so the module could choose a message. Passing a boolean costs us message personalisation and buys us the ability to say, truthfully, that we never see what anyone typed.
Browser · on device

3 · The Shift reads local state and picks a response

Reads localStorage. Exposure 1 → gate + harm message. Exposure 3 → gate + a different message family. Exposure 5+ → form changes: gate becomes a three-turn chat. If the last exposure was under the minimum interval, format degrades to quiet instead of repeating loudly.

All of this happens with the network switched off. The module is fully functional offline after first load — which is also a nice demo.
Browser · render

4 · The gate is drawn, and the results are withheld

Nothing on this screen is personalised, and nothing is scored. The message is one of a small set, attributed to the platform and to Offlimits jointly — because attribution to a credible source is the single strongest design rule in the literature.

Person · five possible exits

5 · One of five outcomes

clicked_help · left_page · dismissed · continued · timeout. Note that dismissed and continued are different things: one means they closed the message, the other means they went back to what they were doing. Conflating them is how this field has flattered itself for four years.

Browser → edge · 8 fields

6 · One non-identifying counter leaves

The payload in §04. The edge function writes it to an hourly aggregate, strips the connection metadata, and does not log the request. No row in any table corresponds to a person.

Failure path: if the counter can't be sent, nothing retries and nothing is buffered. A lost count is cheaper than a queued identifier.
Aggregation · edge

7 · k-suppression before anything is stored

Any cell with fewer than k=10 events in an hour is folded into a coarser bucket or dropped. This is the kit's own rule, kept, and it is the right one. At small platforms most cells will be suppressed — which is honest, and which is why Tier 4 exists for anyone who needs a real answer.

Person → help service

8 · They leave, and we lose them

Clicking help navigates to offlimits.nl/?src=doorway. From this instant Doorway has no involvement whatsoever. There is no tracking pixel, no callback, no redirect through our infrastructure. The service sees an ordinary anonymous visitor.

This is the most important line in the flow. A redirect through our own domain would let us measure arrival — and would hand us the IP addresses of everyone who asked for help. We do not take that trade.
Help service · ordinary operations

9 · The service counts its own contacts

Offlimits counts "how did you hear about us" as it already does. Once a month it sends one number back. No case data, no identifiers, no timestamps.

Platform · its own dashboard

10 · The one-pager

Trigger volume, reach per thousand searches, format mix, click-through with an honest denominator, exposure-bucket curve, arrivals if the partner reports them. Designed to be screenshotted into a funder report.

Collective

11 · A signed, reproducible public feed

Signed monthly aggregate release — the first public dataset of what actually happens when people are warned. Anyone can re-derive the headline numbers from it. This is the artifact UNICRI would actually want, and it is worth more to the field than the module.

Where the flow breaks, and the honest answer

Step 2 is the weak point. In app mode the platform must decide when a trigger fires, which means the platform holds a blocklist and makes a judgement. Many small platforms will get this wrong or never do it — and the Cloudflare edge mode, which solves it, only works for sites behind Cloudflare.

The answer is not to pretend otherwise: ship a curated, maintainable trigger pack alongside the module, accept that hash-of-term lists can be reverse-engineered and say so, and treat "getting the trigger right" as the support burden it is. Every deployment in this literature has this problem and none of them admit it.

06

Benchmark — the ACAPS product, specified

The other half of the comparison, and it is a genuinely different animal: not a thing that interrupts anyone, but a process that turns a sentence into a number and gives the people who wrote it a way to disagree.

One-line definition

A ninety-second elicitation that turns each of ACAPS' own risk statements into a probability, without asking any expert to read more than one card — and a dissent channel that routes the analyst's objections back into the language of the risk, not just its number.

Why this shape

Three things in the ACAPS ground truth force it. The 266 risk blocks all carry dates, which means every statement has a window and every window can be scored. 101 of the 139 scored triggers carry no written resolution criterion, and 31 of 139 risks have no triggers at all, which means the product's job is triage, not coverage. And the expert-forecasting literature is unanimous on the thing that matters: the elicitation format, not the expert, determines the quality — small panels, equal weights, a revision round, absolute proper scoring, and resolution criteria fixed before the question opens.

The three screens

The card

expert · 90 seconds
What one expert sees, once
Layout
One risk statement, verbatim. Its resolution window. The historical base rate for that band. A slider. One optional sentence of rationale. One optional flag.
The discipline
No other statements on screen, no leaderboard, no other experts' numbers before submission. Anchoring and herding are the two best-documented failure modes in expert elicitation and both are caused by the interface.
What the expert is told up front
Where their estimate goes, who sees it, whether their name is attached, and when they will be told what happened. All four must be true before the slider appears.

The analyst desk

analyst · every morning
Where the panel meets the risk
Shows
For each risk: panel median, dispersion, the model baseline, and a divergence flag where they disagree by more than a threshold. Plus the dissent queue — statements an expert believes are badly framed, separate from the estimates.
The one design rule
Dissent is a first-class object, not a comment field. It attaches to the risk statement and can be acted on: rewritten, split, resolved early, or retired. That is the "my dissent changed the paragraph" promise and it has to be a workflow, not a sentiment.

The gate

system · before any card ships
The quality control nobody builds
Checks
Is the resolution window explicit? Is the outcome observable? Does the statement leak its own answer (to be revised upward / further deterioration expected / already deteriorating)? Is it two risks wearing one sentence? Is it a statement of intent rather than a condition?
Why it matters
Every one of those failures makes a forecast meaningless and none of them is caught by asking an expert. The gate is cheap, deterministic, and it is the difference between a forecast and an opinion.

What it deliberately does not do

  • No prediction market. The literature is clear that small panels with equal weights and a revision round match or beat markets at low n, and a market needs liquidity ACAPS does not have.
  • No LLM as the forecaster. o3 at 0.1352 against a Metaculus crowd at 0.149 is real, but superforecasters are at ~0.086 on ForecastBench and parity is projected around mid-2027. An LLM-centric moat has a two-year half-life. A model baseline is useful; a model product is not.
  • No public leaderboard. It converts experts into competitors and destroys the calibration that makes the number worth having.

Benchmark: the information flow, end to end

The expert
  • Their estimate
  • Their rationale text
  • Their dissent, if any
  • Their identity — held once
ACAPS
  • The risk statements — already theirs
  • Which experts were invited
  • The panel's aggregate
  • The resolved outcomes
Public
  • k-suppressed aggregates
  • Calibration over time
  • No rationale text
  • No individual estimates
ACAPS · export

1 · The Risk Radar export arrives

266 blocks with dates, countries, severity bands and the statement text. Ingested as-is. Nothing is inferred, nothing is enriched.

Parser

2 · Statements are split, deduplicated, decontaminated

One statement per card. Near-duplicates collapsed so one risk is not counted four times. Outcome-leaking language flagged and removed from the card the expert sees, while the original is preserved for audit.

Decontamination is not cosmetic. A statement that says "further deterioration expected" has already told the expert the answer. Forecasting it produces a good Brier score and no information.
Gate

3 · Each card passes or is returned

Resolution window? Observable outcome? One risk rather than two? A condition rather than an intention? Anything that fails goes back to the analyst with a reason, not silently into the pool.

Model

4 · The number is computed before anyone is asked

A completion-rate baseline from the historical data — this is the part that already exists and already works. It is shown to the expert alongside the historical base rate, so the human is never guessing at a base rate the model already knows.

Order matters. Computing before eliciting means the expert's job is to adjust a number, not to invent one. That is the difference between a 90-second task and a 9-minute one, and task length is the single biggest driver of non-completion in expert elicitation.
Expert · 90 seconds

5 · One card, one slider, one optional sentence

No peers' numbers visible. No leaderboard. The expert sees who will see their name, when they will be told the outcome, and what happens if they disagree with the framing.

Aggregation

6 · Equal weights, then a revision round

Median and dispersion. A second pass where each expert sees the distribution — but not who said what — and may revise. Two rounds maximum; the literature finds little gain beyond that and completion falls fast.

Analyst

7 · The desk opens

Median, spread, model baseline, divergence flags, dissent queue. The analyst sees names. Experts see only aggregates. This asymmetry is the entire trust model and it must be stated before the first card is sent.

Dissent channel

8 · Objections attach to the sentence

"This is two risks." "The window is wrong." "This is already happening." A dissent is routed to the analyst as a structured object with the statement attached, and the analyst can rewrite, split, or retire. The expert is told what happened to it.

The loop-closing is the product. An expert who dissents into silence does not dissent twice. This is the retention mechanism the whole system depends on.
Resolution

9 · The window closes and the outcome is observed

Observed, not inferred, wherever possible. Anything unobservable is recorded as unobservable rather than scored as a miss — a rule that has to be set before the first card or the Brier score becomes a measure of ambition.

Scoring

10 · Brier, calibration, and a confidence interval

Per-expert and panel-level. Publicly, only the panel. With a bootstrap interval attached, every time — see §09 for why this is non-negotiable.

Feedback

11 · The expert is told what happened

Their own score, their own calibration curve, and what their dissent did. This is the cheapest retention mechanism in the literature and almost nobody implements it.

Export

12 · Back into ACAPS' workflow

The number returned alongside the sentence it belongs to, with the analyst's note. The output is not a dashboard — it is a slightly better Risk Radar paragraph, six weeks later.

08

Audit: does Doorway make sense?

Nine findings, ordered by how much damage each does if unaddressed. The first is fatal if ignored and cheap to fix; the last is a footnote.

Fatal → fixed

F1 · The choice-architecture thesis contradicts the plug-and-play premise. The strongest finding in the research is that removing alternatives produces a 700× difference. But a plug-in cannot remove a platform's alternatives — it can only gate what comes back from one search, on a site that remains fully functional behind the gate. So the headline claim cannot be delivered by the product, and a jury member who has read the Ahmia paper will see it in the first ninety seconds.

The fix

Narrow the claim from outcome to mechanism. Don't say "we get Ahmia-like rates." Say: "the evidence says the format that removes alternatives wins by two orders of magnitude; a plug-in can't remove the platform's alternatives, so it does the next best thing — it withholds the result until the person acknowledges it. That is a gate, not a banner, and it is the most any installable module can do. We are testing whether gate beats banner, and nobody has ever tested that." That is a smaller claim and a much stronger one, because it is testable and unclaimed.

Fatal → fixed

F2 · The demo would otherwise use the organiser's baked 15% rate. tools/generate-traffic.js ships with 15% and 18% click-through against a 1% control. No real deployment has produced that on a general-purpose platform. A dashboard showing a 15× lift is the most likely way for this submission to lose.

The fix

Build against honest baselines: 0.5–1.5% pre-intervention, 2–3× lift expected, and say the number is a hypothesis. Then show the plugin actually generates those rates by running it on the mock platform at realistic volume. Turning the weakest slide into the credibility slide is worth more than any feature.

Serious → design around it

F3 · Success breaks the help service. Offlimits took 943 contacts in an entire year — about 2.6 a day for 17.9 million people. A module installed across a few hundred platforms at even Intercept's ~1% rate would produce more demand than the service can absorb, and the "route to a human" step that the whole product is built on would collapse.

The resolution, and its cost

Default to self-help (Stoppen is mogelijk, ReDirection), route to human channels only on signals of higher need, and give the service a forecast. But be honest about what that costs: the evidence says human contact is where the effect is, and the capacity says most people cannot have it. That tension is real and unresolvable at hackathon scale. Name it in the pitch rather than papering over it — a team that names it looks like the only team that thought about the system rather than the screen.

Serious → design around it

F4 · "Cheap, easy, compelling" and "measure it properly" pull in opposite directions. The reason the install is four minutes is that it commits the platform to nothing. A stepped-wedge trial commits it to nine weeks and two rounds of data collection. Most platforms will take the free widget and decline the study.

The resolution

Ship them as two products with one codebase: the free module (zero commitment, tiers 1–2) and study mode (opt-in, tier 4). Then in the pitch, be explicit that study mode is the part that would actually answer the question the field has failed to answer — and that the reason it exists separately is that forcing it would halve adoption. That is a coherent product decision, and coherence is what the audit is for.

Real → accept and state

F5 · The product is not provably effective inside a weekend, and never will be. The most recent systematic review concludes it is "not possible to draw any broader conclusions as to the impacts of these digital interventions at this time." Ahmia randomised 3.1 million warnings over 140 days and found no detectable reduction in searching.

The resolution

Stop claiming deterrence. Claim interruption and routing, which are what the evidence actually supports, and which are what the product actually does. Then say: "the honest answer to 'does it work' is that nobody knows, which is why we ship the protocol alongside the product." Nobody else in the room will say that, and it is the single most differentiating sentence available.

Real → structural

F6 · Voluntary adoption at the tail has never worked at scale in this field. The one platform that changed did so because Mastercard and Visa suspended payments — not because anyone proved a warning worked. There is no payment rail at a 20,000-member forum, and the EU regulation that would create one is not law.

The resolution

Don't argue that conscience will scale. Argue that the artifact must exist before the mandate arrives, and that the tail is the segment the incumbent consultancy structurally cannot serve. Then use Tech Coalition Pathways, the Dutch Safer Internet Centre and the UNICRI hub as the distribution channels, because they already have the platforms' attention. This turns the weakest assumption into an explicit bet, which is a much better position than an implicit one.

Minor → decide once

F7 · Five formats is a configuration surface that contradicts "easy." Every extra option is a decision a moderator can get wrong.

Resolution: formats are never user-selected. The platform picks a type (forum, marketplace, chat, search) during setup and the Shift chooses format automatically from there. The settings screen should have exactly two fields and one of them should be pre-filled.

Minor → decide once

F8 · The trigger pack is the real support burden, and it is a list of terms. Shipping a blocklist means shipping something that can be read, scraped, and reverse-engineered — and it means maintaining it by language and by slang.

Resolution: ship term hashes rather than plaintext, accept that this is obfuscation rather than security, and say so in the docs. And distribute the pack privately as a signed bundle to verified installs only — never in the public repository, never shown in the dashboard. My first version of this said "community-maintainable", which breaks the brief's explicit safety rule against public trigger lists; see §12 for the corrected design.

Minor → ignore

F9 · The badge could be gamed by a platform that installs and never configures.

Resolution: the status endpoint already solves it. Two states, live or absent, plus a minimum-trigger requirement before the badge appears at all. A site with the module installed and zero triggers in 30 days shows nothing.

Verdict on Doorway

The premise holds, the mechanism is buildable, and the two fatal findings are both fixable by narrowing claims rather than adding features. The unresolved risks — F3 and F6 — are structural properties of the field, not defects in the design, and the correct move is to name them rather than solve them. What would kill it: presenting the 700× number as an expected outcome, or shipping a dashboard that flatters.

09

Audit: does Benchmark make sense?

Eight findings. The first is the one that would end the track if a sharp juror found it, and it is the reason the ACAPS recommendation in this document is more cautious than the one in unicri-research.html.

Fatal → unresolved

A1 · The bar to clear is ACAPS' own 0.2168. The ladder, measured in this repo: naive floor 0.2425 → ACAPS' own ratings 0.2168 → a logistic regression on the same fields 0.2177 out of sample. Lower Brier is better — so the regression does not beat the analysts. An earlier revision claimed 0.1981 as "the bar" and had the ordering backwards; the number was not reproducible and the model was fitting Risk Level, which is a pure function of Probability on 125/125 rows. Every pitch that says "we beat ACAPS' 0.2204" is beating a number that does not exist. The panel has to beat ACAPS, and no experiment has shown that it does.

Why this is not fixed by better framing

It is a genuine empirical question that the weekend cannot answer, because answering it requires the panel to have run and the outcomes to have resolved. There are three honest positions and only one of them is a product:

  • Displacement — "the model is the product, the panel adds interpretability." Coherent, but then it is a modelling submission, not an expert-elicitation one, and the concept-space differentiation collapses.
  • Complement — "the panel's value is divergence from the model, not accuracy." Also coherent, but it cannot be scored, so it cannot be the headline.
  • The honest one — "we do not know yet, here is the pre-registered test, and here is why the panel should beat .1981: it sees the sentence, and the four fields do not." That is the argument, and it is a good one, but it must be made as a hypothesis with a design, not as a result.

This is why the ACAPS track is higher-risk than it looked on Wednesday. Its central claim is unproven in a way that cannot be proven in 48 hours, while Doorway's central claim is unproven in a way that is at least honestly unproven for everybody.

Fatal → fixed

A2 · With 104 resolved risks, no Brier difference in this range is statistically distinguishable without a confidence interval. Measured at n≈104: the standard error on the mean Brier is 0.0084 for the constant base rate and 0.0184 for ACAPS' rating; the standard error of the paired difference is 0.0148 (npm run ladder). The gap between ACAPS' 0.2168 and the regression's 0.2177 is 0.0009 — far inside even the paired interval, which is the whole reason the ladder must be paired. An earlier revision put this standard error at 0.04–0.05; that was wrong by 2–5×. A juror who asks "what is your interval" and gets a shrug has caught the whole submission.

The fix, and it is cheap

Bootstrap it. 10,000 resamples, report the interval on every score, and report a paired difference (same risks, two methods) rather than two independent scores — the paired comparison has far more power because the errors are correlated. Then state the power honestly: at n=104 you can detect a large effect, not a small one. Attaching an interval is a half-day of work and it converts the weakest point in the ACAPS submission into a demonstration of rigour. Do this even if the interval is embarrassing, because the embarrassment is smaller than being caught.

Serious → fixed by wording

A3 · "Nobody knows it was me" is not true, and the promise is the product. The analyst sees names. ACAPS holds the invitation list. If the pitch promises anonymity and the product delivers pseudonymity-to-peers, the first expert in the room will catch it — and ACAPS is a room full of people who care about exactly this.

The fix

Change the promise to the true and stronger one: "your peers never see your name, and your dissent is never attributed in the output." That is what the product does, it is what experts actually need (retribution comes from peers, not from the commissioning body), and it is a claim you can defend under questioning. Never promise anonymity to the organisation that is paying for the forecast.

Serious → test on site

A4 · Every ACAPS claim depends on experts agreeing to do 90-second tasks, repeatedly, for no money. The forecast-tournament literature is unforgiving here: the X-risk tournament saw 51% expert dropout against 19% for superforecasters, and the RAND RFI programme was eventually shut down. The 10-of-60 response rate to ACAPS' own expert survey is the number to beat, but there is no evidence that experts recruited for this task will beat it.

Resolution: this is a Friday conversation, not a build decision. Find the ACAPS team on site, ask what their actual response rate is to internal requests for judgement, and treat the answer as a gating input. If the answer is "we struggle to get them to fill in the matrix," that is not a reason to abandon the track — it is the reason the product exists — but it does mean the pitch must lead with the recruitment design rather than the interface.

Real → ambiguity

A5 · It is not settled whether the ACAPS challenge wants a product or an analysis. The brief and the evaluation criteria are the ground truth here and I have read them, but the two readings have very different implications: if the challenge wants analysis, a tool is off-brief and a well-argued methodology is on-brief.

Resolution: re-read the evaluation criteria before the first commit and quote the operative sentence in the pitch. Do not assume; the difference between the two readings is the difference between entering the right competition and the wrong one.

Minor → keep

A6 · Three rubrics are in circulation for this challenge. Different teams will optimise for different ones and the jury will use one.

Resolution: pitch the union. The overlapping requirement across all three is that the concept addresses the brief's own stated problem rather than an adjacent one, so every slide should be traceable to a sentence in the brief.

Minor → decide once

A7 · Decontamination removes language the analyst considered essential. Stripping "further deterioration expected" makes for a cleaner forecast and a worse risk statement.

Resolution: keep both versions. The expert sees the clean one; the analyst and the export keep the original. The product's job is to preserve the analyst's sentence, not to edit it — and that distinction should be visible in the interface, because it is the thing ACAPS will actually care about.

Minor → ignore

A8 · The scoring rule can be gamed by forecasting 0 or 1. A proper scoring rule is only proper if the resolution is honest.

Resolution: "unobservable is not a miss" must be fixed before the first card, in writing, and shown to the experts. It removes the incentive to hedge toward certainty and it is the single cheapest piece of methodological credibility available.

Verdict on Benchmark

The premise holds and the method is sound, but the central claim is unproven against the benchmark that actually matters, and one of the two fatal findings cannot be closed inside the weekend. A1 and A2 are both fixable as framing and statistics — bootstrap the intervals, move the claim from result to hypothesis, and argue the specific mechanism by which a panel beats four fields (it reads the sentence). That is a legitimate and even attractive submission. But it is a submission that says "we will know in six weeks", not one that says "here is the number." Which is exactly the opposite of Doorway's risk profile, and exactly why the two tracks cannot be run by the same five people on the same weekend.
